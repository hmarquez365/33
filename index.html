02 — THE RELATIONSHIP

Good agriculture
needs good partners.

A resilient supply chain is built over time. Clear expectations, knowledge-sharing and appropriate tools can help producers make decisions with a longer horizon.

2010Barilla Sustainable Farming programme launched
~7,000farmers involved across strategic supply chains in 2025
816,000 tcereals and basil sourced through the programme in 2025

Group-wide figures for cereals and basil across four supply chains — not durum-wheat-only figures. Source: Barilla 2025 Sustainability Report.

01

Fairer, clearer relationships

Transparent specifications and dialogue can make expectations clearer for both producers and buyers. The quality and sustainability requirements need to be understood along the chain.

02

Planning over time

Cultivation contracts and defined agronomic requirements can support production planning. They do not remove weather, market or crop risks, but can make some decisions more predictable.

03

Tools and innovation

Digital agronomic platforms, crop-variety research and field data can help farmers assess conditions and make more informed choices.

04

Training and expertise

Technical exchange, research partnerships and practical guidance can strengthen professional skills and support the adoption of improved practices.

What this does — and does not — mean

These are mechanisms through which agricultural partnerships can create value. Barilla publicly describes cultivation contracts, agronomic specifications, digital support and collaboration with farmers. This page does not claim that every farmer receives direct financing, a guaranteed price or identical contract terms.

04 — THE FUTURE

The soil remembers
every decision.

Sustainable agriculture is not one technique. It is a set of choices adapted to local conditions, aimed at protecting productive land over the long term.

01 / 04

Soil health & biodiversity

Crop rotation, organic matter and habitat diversity can support soil structure and living ecosystems.

≈
02 / 04

Water stewardship

Monitoring, efficient irrigation where used and attention to soil moisture help manage a limited resource.

↻
03 / 04

Regenerative practices

Cover crops, reduced tillage and diversified rotations are examples that may improve soil function when suited to local conditions.

☼
04 / 04

Climate resilience

Locally adapted varieties, weather information and agronomic research can help farms respond to changing conditions.
